DWI (Driving While Intoxicated)

A DWI arrest in Texas starts two separate cases, and most people only find out about the second one after it is too late to do anything about it.

The license case (ALR)

Administrative License Revocation is handled by the Department of Public Safety, not the criminal court. A written hearing request filed inside the deadline puts the suspension on hold until a judge hears it — and the hearing is a chance to question the arresting officer under oath, months before trial.

The criminal case

Charges range from a Class B misdemeanor for a first offense to a felony where there was a child passenger, a serious injury, or prior convictions. The stop, the field sobriety testing, and the breath or blood evidence each have procedures behind them, and procedure is where these cases are won.

Keeping you driving

If a suspension is coming, an occupational license can often keep you driving to work, school, and appointments. That is a separate filing, and worth starting early.
